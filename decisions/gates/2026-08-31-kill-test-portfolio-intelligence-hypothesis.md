# Kill Test — Portfolio Intelligence / Institutional Project Graph

Research date: 2026-08-31
Status: `KILL_AS_VENTURE_HYPOTHESIS / INTERNAL-UTILITY-ONLY-CANDIDATE`

## Hypothesis under test

> A system that connects many internally owned projects, repositories, features, capabilities, research assets, dependencies, evidence, and decisions can become a sufficiently differentiated and valuable independent venture, or a general product that should be built for external customers.

## Adversarial findings

### 1. Enterprise portfolio management is already a mature category

Planview positions Strategic Portfolio Management as a continuously updated model connecting initiatives, investments, capacity, execution, outcomes, technology, and financials. Its 2026 product set includes AI-powered prioritization, scenario comparison, portfolio analytics, capability-based planning, dependency intelligence, and an Outcome Intelligence Graph. Planview reports more than 3,000 enterprise customers and substantial portfolio deployments.

Growth Forge positions itself as enterprise innovation portfolio management, combining configurable evaluation criteria, comparable financial models, portfolio visibility, and evidence-driven innovation investment decisions.

Aha! and other product portfolio management systems already connect portfolio strategy, products, roadmaps, dependencies, and execution.

**Assessment:** generic portfolio intelligence is not an empty category.

### 2. Project/repository graph intelligence is also crowded

Public systems and research already implement repository knowledge graphs, dependency mapping, call graphs, impact analysis, semantic retrieval, and temporal project memory. Examples include Code Graph Knowledge System, ARIA, Lattice, RepoDoc, and SemRepo.

**Assessment:** repository graph + AI analysis + dependency intelligence is not sufficient differentiation.

### 3. Venture studios are independently building shared operating systems

Multiple 2026 venture studios describe centralized operating platforms, shared infrastructure, reusable capabilities, institutional memory, portfolio-wide learning, and AI-assisted venture operations. Examples include Averian Labs' Orbit, Tsalach's operating system, Galactic's shared platform, and other studios using shared infrastructure across portfolio companies.

**Assessment:** the idea that a multi-project organization benefits from a shared intelligence/operating layer is already being pursued in practice.

### 4. The proposed internal system has a strong internal-use case, but that is not venture proof

For a portfolio containing hundreds of repositories, linking projects, assets, capabilities, dependencies, evidence, and decisions may reduce duplicate work and improve discovery. However, this is currently an internal productivity thesis.

There is no direct evidence yet that external organizations want this exact product from a new vendor rather than established SPM/PPM tools, developer intelligence platforms, venture-studio operating systems, or internal systems.

**Assessment:** internal utility may be real; external venture demand is unproven.

### 5. User-specific portfolio complexity is not a moat

A system optimized for one organization's unusual repository history, project taxonomy, and research corpus may be highly useful internally but difficult to generalize. The more customization required, the stronger the case becomes for an internal tool and the weaker the case for a scalable product.

**Assessment:** current differentiation is contextual, not durable market defensibility.

### 6. AI reasoning is not sufficient differentiation

LLMs can already summarize projects, suggest relationships, identify duplicate concepts, and generate prioritization narratives. The competitive bar is therefore not "AI can understand our projects" but whether a system creates an indispensable workflow, proprietary data asset, switching cost, or unique distribution advantage.

**Assessment:** AI analysis alone does not rescue the product thesis.

## What remains true

The following internal capabilities may still be valuable:

- repository and artifact inventory;
- capability registry;
- dependency and relationship graph;
- semantic duplicate detection;
- project-to-project composition discovery;
- evidence-linked project state;
- reusable-asset extraction;
- historical decision/outcome tracking.

These should be treated as possible internal engineering capabilities, not automatically as a company opportunity.

## Decision

### External venture/product thesis

# `KILL`

The broad hypothesis "build a portfolio intelligence platform for organizations with many projects" does not currently clear the venture-gap bar. Existing SPM/PPM vendors, repository intelligence systems, and venture studios already occupy substantial portions of the proposed capability space.

### Internal tooling thesis

# `KEEP OPEN`

An internal system may still have unusually high value because the organization can exploit its own repository corpus and does not need external WTP. This must be evaluated as an internal ROI/engineering productivity experiment, not as a startup hypothesis.

## Conditions required to reopen as a venture

Reopen only if new evidence identifies a narrow, repeated external workflow that:

1. established SPM/PPM systems do not solve adequately;
2. repository/code intelligence tools do not solve adequately;
3. venture-studio operating systems do not solve adequately;
4. internal build is materially more expensive than buying;
5. has a clear economic buyer and budget;
6. has a defensible advantage beyond LLM reasoning and data aggregation.
